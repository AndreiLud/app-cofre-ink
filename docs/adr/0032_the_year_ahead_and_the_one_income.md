# ADR 0032: The year ahead, the one income, and what standing still costs

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 23 September 2026
**Deciders:** Andrei Ludescher (owner), Claude (implementation)

## Context

Three things were named as still missing from the check up, and the owner asked for all
three: what happens if the income stops, the bills that come once a year, and what idle
money costs while it sits there.

Each one has a trap, and the traps are the decision.

## Decision

### Where the money comes from, without the sermon

Almost every household answers with one word, and there is nothing wrong with that.
Somebody with one job is not doing anything badly, so this does not scold and does not
suggest anybody find a second income.

What it states is the fact and the one consequence that follows: **if the biggest source
stopped today, what is on hand covers so many months of ordinary spending.** With a
single source that number is the reserve again, which is the point of saying it that way:
the reserve stops being a rule of thumb and becomes the answer to a question people have
actually asked themselves at three in the morning.

A source is padded with the months it brought nothing. A bit of freelance that landed
twice in six months has a median of zero and does not count as income, because counting
it would say the household is less exposed than it is.

### A year of months, read separately from the six

A bill that comes once a year cannot be seen in six months of records, so the totals are
now read over eighteen months. Only this question uses them: **every median in the package
is still made of the six**, because what an ordinary month costs today should not be
decided by last year. The six are the first six of the eighteen, from one query.

A month is dearer when it went a fifth above an ordinary month of that year, and what it
produces is the only thing worth producing about a lump in the future: a monthly figure.
A thousand in February is frightening. Two hundred a month starting now is a decision.

Below twelve closed months the panel is not drawn at all. With eight months of records,
the four that are missing are exactly the ones nobody can see coming.

### What standing still costs, and nothing about where to put it

The finding that says there is money beyond the reserve now carries what a year of
inflation takes out of it, from the IPCA already cached for offline use, compounded over
the twelve published months.

The line is drawn exactly where ADR 0029 drew it. Saying what inflation does to money
that sits still is arithmetic about their own balance. Saying where to put it instead is
not, and this does not. No product is named, no rate is recommended, and no comparison
with any investment is offered.

When nobody has fetched the index, which is the ordinary case offline and on a fresh
install, the sentence without it is used. A figure about somebody's money that depends on
a number nobody has is better left unsaid than guessed.

## Consequences

1. The Snapshot grew three more series: income by source and month, the longer window of
   totals, and the inflation of the last twelve published months.
2. The check up is now eight panels, plus the verdict and the note on how it was worked
   out. It is a report and reads like one. This is the point at which the length becomes
   worth watching: the next thing added should probably replace something.
3. Income sources are grouped by the description as written, the same bargain the
   repeating charges make. A salary written three ways is three sources, and the rules
   that tidy descriptions are the means to fix that.
4. The inflation figure is the first number on this screen that did not come out of the
   household's own records. It is public, it is cached, it is named on the screen, and it
   is the only one.
